[O] Take us through the method properly. What actually happens when I hand it a prompt?
[G] One GPT-3 completion does the entire front end, which was a deliberate design choice. Given the text, the model performs three steps inside a single inference. First, element extraction: pull out noun phrases including named entities, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, and short parse-tree spans of no more than three words. For a photo of three dogs, the elements are photo, three, and dogs.
[S] Three elements from six words.
[G] Yes, and the second step classifies each element into one of twelve fixed categories. Object, activity, animal, food, counting, color, material, spatial, location, shape, attribute, and other. Three becomes counting. Dogs becomes animal. Photo becomes attribute. Then the third step generates exactly two questions per element, and the pairing is the clever part.
[O] Why two?
[G] Because they are deliberately different shapes. The first is binary, and it is written so the correct answer for a faithful image is yes. Are there three dogs? Yes or no. The second is multiple choice with the element itself as the gold answer. How many dogs are in the photo? One, two, three, four. Answer, three. The authors say this makes the evaluation robust to surface-level differences, and I think that is right. A model that has learned to say yes to everything gets caught by the second form. A model that fumbles the choice list gets caught by the first.
[S] And all of that is one API call.
[G] One inference, temperature zero, using the code-davinci engine, conditioned on fifteen hand-annotated in-context examples that are identical for every prompt in the benchmark. That is the cost control. Human annotation is a fixed fifteen examples, not a per-prompt expense.
[O] Then there is a filter stage, which I think is underrated.
[G] It is, and it is stricter than people assume. UnifiedQA is asked the same question twice. Once as multiple choice, once free-form. The pair survives only if the multiple-choice answer matches GPT-3's gold answer, and the word-level F1 between the free-form answer and the gold answer is above point seven. Both conditions. Note what is being checked here: whether the question is answerable from the text alone. It is a text-side consistency check, not an image-side one.
[S] What does that filter actually buy?
[G] They audited it. A thousand filtered question-answer pairs went to human annotators, and only seven were judged unreasonable.
