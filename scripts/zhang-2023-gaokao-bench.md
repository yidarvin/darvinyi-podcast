[S] It is on the docket because it is one of the earliest benchmarks that took subjective, human-graded, free-response questions seriously, at a moment when essentially everyone else was counting multiple-choice accuracy. It has picked up a couple of hundred citations since.
[O] And joining us is Verity, who has been through this one and its appendices closely. Welcome.
[G] Thank you. One housekeeping note before we start, because it is genuinely unusual. The byline runs to fourteen names across two lines. The first six carry affiliation numbers and markers — three equal-contribution asterisks and two corresponding-author daggers. The eight names on the second line carry no marker, no affiliation number, and there is no acknowledgement section explaining the group. Indexing services list only six authors. I am not going to speculate about why, but I am flagging it so nobody assumes the shorter author list they have seen elsewhere is what the paper itself prints.
[S] Noted. Set the scene for me. Mid-2023, what did the benchmark landscape actually look like?
[G] Crowded, and converging on one shape. MMLU, BIG-Bench and HELM on the English side. On the Chinese side, C-Eval, AGIEval and CMMLU had all arrived within months of each other. The authors' complaint about every one of them is a single sentence, and it is the whole thesis of the paper: those benchmarks, in their words, only consist of objective questions and lack subjective questions that are more closely related to generative abilities.
[O] Multiple choice is a proxy. You measure whether a model can pick A, B, C or D, and you infer that it can write.
[G] Right. Their second complaint is subtler and I think more interesting. They argue that in the absence of real-world test samples, people underestimate both how hard the tasks are and how capable the models are. A question set authored for a benchmark drifts away from what a genuinely high-stakes exam looks like.
[S] So rather than design a harder format, they take one off the shelf.
[G] They take the GAOKAO. It already mixes both kinds of question by design, it is expert-vetted, it is national, and it comes with a scoring scheme that has real consequences attached. And prior GAOKAO-derived benchmarks were much narrower — the paper describes them as mainly focused on English, that is Yuan and Liu in 2022, and especially on English reading comprehension, Zhang and colleagues in 2022. Nobody had done the whole exam.
[O] So the novelty is total coverage plus the subjective half. Give me the dataset.
[G] Every national GAOKAO paper from 2010 to 2022, across nine subjects — Chinese, English, mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, politics, history, geography. Mathematics appears twice in every table because the sciences track and the humanities track sit different maths papers. 2,811 questions in total: 1,781 objective and 1,030 subjective. They converted them out of PDF into JSON with a mix of scripting and manual annotation, and hand-converted the mathematics into LaTeX.
[S] Break the two halves down for me.
[G] The objective half is 1,418 single-choice, 273 multi-question-choice — that is one passage carrying several sub-questions — 64 multi-choice, meaning more than one correct answer, and 26 in a five-out-of-seven format, which is the English cloze test. The subjective half is 786 open-ended, 218 cloze, and 26 error-correction items.
[O] And the grading?
[G] Two completely different regimes, and keeping them apart is most of the work of reading this paper. Objective questions are graded automatically by regular-expression matching of the model's stated answer against the standard answer — they explicitly follow the approach OpenAI describes in the GPT-4 technical report. Subjective questions are graded by two human teachers per question, and the average of the two scores is the final score for that item.
[S] Then everything gets folded into one number.
[G] Yes, and this is where marks and percentages get confused, so let me be precise. Every subject has a fixed objective mark total and a fixed subjective mark total, weighted to match the GAOKAO's own historical proportions. Chinese is 45 objective marks and 105 subjective, for 150. English is the mirror image — 105 objective and 45 subjective, also 150. Each maths paper is 60 and 90. Physics is 44 and 66, for 110. Chemistry, politics and history are 50 and 50, for 100 each. Biology is 27 and 63, for 90. Geography is 40 and 60, for 100. Sum the six sciences-track subjects and you get exactly 750. Sum the six humanities-track subjects and you also get exactly 750.
[S] That English weighting is doing enormous work. English is the one subject where the objective side carries more than twice the marks of the subjective side.
[G] It is, and it maps directly onto where GPT-4 is strongest. Hold that thought for the results.
[O] What about prompting?
[G] Zero-shot, with prompts that specify the required output format as well as the task, because the authors treat intrinsic knowledge and instruction-following as equally important — they say that explicitly. Sampling temperature is 0.3 for the models under test. The output format is quite rigid: the model writes its reasoning between an analysis marker and an end-of-explanation token, and its answer between an answer marker and an end-of-answer token.
[S] Now the model list. And I want to be careful here, because I think the paper's twelve models are really two different experiments wearing one coat.
[G] That is correct, and it is the single most important structural fact about this paper. Twelve models appear somewhere in it. Only five — GPT-4-0613, GPT-4-0314, GPT-3.5-turbo-0301, ERNIE-Bot-0615 and ERNIE-Bot-turbo-0725 — receive human-graded subjective scores, and therefore only those five ever get a converted total out of 750. The other seven — LLaMA-7b, Vicuna-7b, three Baichuan 2 variants and two ChatGLM variants — are scored on objective questions only.
[O] Because hiring teachers does not scale.
[G] Because hiring teachers does not scale, and the paper says so plainly in its Limitations. I want to give them credit for that. They write that due to the rapid development of language models and the high cost of human evaluation, they were unable to run human scoring on every model. It is disclosed, not buried.
[S] Disclosed and still consequential. We will come back to it.
[O] There is a third experiment though, and it is the one that aged best. The judge.
[G] Yes. Since human grading does not scale, they had the teachers write detailed marking criteria that decompose each answer into discrete scoring points, then used GPT-4-turbo — the 1106-preview checkpoint, temperature zero — as the grader. The judge sees the question, the standard answer, the mark value, the official analysis, the marking criteria, and the model's reasoning and answer. It outputs a scoring rationale and a final score. They then correlate the judge's per-question scores against the teachers' averaged scores.
[S] Right. Numbers. Start with the headline.
[G] Converted totals, human-graded, out of 750 per track. GPT-4-0314 leads both — 447 on sciences, 485 on humanities. GPT-4-0613 is just behind at 434 and 480. ERNIE-Bot-0615 gets 333 and 397. GPT-3.5-turbo-0301 gets 300 and 368. ERNIE-Bot-turbo-0725 is last at 274 and 306.
[O] So ERNIE-Bot beats ChatGPT on both tracks. On a Chinese exam, in mid-2023. That is a genuinely notable result and I do not think it got enough attention at the time.
[G] And every single model scores higher on humanities than on sciences, without exception. The authors attribute that to the sciences demanding more logical reasoning and multi-step computation, while the humanities reward breadth of knowledge.
[S] What does the per-subject breakdown look like? I assume the aggregate is hiding a great deal.
[G] It is hiding almost everything. Take GPT-4-0613 on objective questions — 93.2 percent in English, 83.0 percent in biology, 81.1 percent in geography, and then 43.6 percent in chemistry and 50.8 percent in physics. On subjective questions the same model gets 87.6 percent in English and 24.6 percent on sciences mathematics.
[O] That is a sixty-three point spread, in percentage points, inside one model, on one grading regime.
[G] Yes. And the language asymmetry cuts both ways. ERNIE-Bot-0615 scores 31.0 percent on objective English, against GPT-4-0613's 93.2. But on subjective Chinese, ERNIE-Bot-0615 gets 57.1 percent, ahead of GPT-4-0314's 51.5.
[S] Each model wins its home language.
[G] Broadly, yes. And ERNIE-Bot-0615 also takes the top closed-source objective score in three subjects — chemistry, 66.1 percent against GPT-4-0314's 44.4; politics, 86.9 against GPT-4-0613's 72.5; and history, 79.1 against GPT-4-0314's 75.6. Those are exactly the three subjects the paper's own text calls out.
[O] And the open-source seven?
[G] Objective only, so the ceiling is a different ceiling. Baichuan2-13b-Chat is best at 43.9 percent overall, then ChatGLM2-6b at 42.7, Baichuan2-7b-Chat at 40.5, ChatGLM-6b at 30.8, Baichuan2-7b-Base at 27.2, and then LLaMA-7b at 21.1 and Vicuna-7b at 21.0.
[S] Twenty-one percent, on a set that is four-fifths single-choice, is roughly what you get by guessing.
[G] Roughly. Although note the paper never reports a random baseline — there is no chance-level row in any table, which is itself a small gap when your bottom two models are down there. [O] Zero? Not near zero?
[G] Exactly zero, both of them. Now let me step beyond what the paper says and flag that I am doing it. Physics is the one subject whose entire objective set is the multi-choice format — questions with more than one correct answer. The appendix lists physics as 64 multi-choice questions, and the main table's multi-choice total is also 64. Those are the same 64 questions.
[S] So the two models that score zero score zero on the one format that requires emitting a set of answers rather than a single letter, under a regular-expression grader.
[G] That is my read, and the paper does not test it. It never separates a wrong answer from an unparseable one, anywhere. And Baichuan2-7b-Base is clearly not globally broken — it gets 55.3 percent on politics — so it is not that the model is producing nothing at all.

[S] And on absolute score?
[G] Worse, and asymmetrically so. The paper says the deviation is less than two percent of the total on the sciences and around five percent on the humanities. The direction is the part that matters. [O] And on the sciences?
[G] Mixed, which I find more informative than the paper makes it. The judge marks both GPT-4 checkpoints and ERNIE-Bot-turbo slightly down — 434 becomes 428, 447 becomes 440, 274 becomes 262 — and marks GPT-3.5 and ERNIE-Bot-0615 slightly up. So the judge is not uniformly generous. It is generous on open-ended humanities answers specifically.
[S] Which is where semantic partial credit lives.
[G] That is the authors' own explanation. Sciences answers and their criteria are explicit; humanities scoring depends on matching a student's semantics onto designated points.
[O] I want to flag the thing that survives all of this, because it is the practically useful finding. The ranking is preserved. GPT-4-0314 is first on both tracks under both graders.
[G] That is fair, and it is the right way to state the result. The judge is usable for ordering models. It is not usable for reporting a score out of 750.
[S] One caveat on the phrasing. The humanities deviations actually run from two point nine percent up to six point seven percent. "Around five percent" flattens a spread of nearly four points. It is not a false claim — "around" is not a point estimate — but it reads tidier than the data is.
[G] Agreed. And there is a nice illustration of the leniency sitting in the appendix. They walk through one geography essay. The official marking criterion reads: any three points, ten marks. Now, the paper does not report what the two teachers gave that same answer, so this is an illustration and not evidence. But it is a very clean picture of how a criteria-following judge behaves when the student answer is long and the criterion is a menu.
[O] What did the ablation show?
[G] That the criteria carry the result. Without them, GPT-4-0613's Spearman drops from point eight five four to point eight two zero, and its Kendall's tau from point seven one zero to point six five nine. All six comparisons — three models, two statistics — move the same direction. So the human labour did not disappear. It moved from grading every answer to writing a rubric for every question.
[S] Good. Contamination. This is a decade of published exam papers.
[G] The paper takes it seriously and its answer has two parts. First, it argues that the zero-shot setting and the human evaluation alleviate the problem. Second, and this is the substantive part, since the GAOKAO is held every June they plan to fold each new year's questions into the benchmark as a supplement, in order to mitigate leakage. [O] And?
