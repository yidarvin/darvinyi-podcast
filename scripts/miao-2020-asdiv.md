[S] Now the metric. That is the piece that outlived the corpus, so take your time with it.
[G] It is called lexicon usage diversity, L D, and it is defined per problem. First they normalize hard: tokenize and part-of-speech tag with Stanford Core N L P, lemmatize with N L T K, strip stopwords, and replace person names and quantity values with meta symbols. So two problems that differ only in the names or the numbers become literally the same string. Then for a given problem, they find its single most similar other problem, compute BLEU in both directions with n-grams up to four, average the two directions, and subtract that from one.
[O] So it is one minus your nearest neighbour's similarity to you.
[G] Precisely. Zero means you have a twin in the corpus. One means nothing in the corpus resembles you at all. The mean of that across every problem is the corpus lexicon diversity, C L D. [O] Where do you come out overall, Bennett?
[G] The diversity metric is solid, and the Math Q A audit is convincing on its own terms. The corpus is a reasonable artifact. The causal claim, that low diversity is what inflates accuracy, is the weakest link, because the paper's cleanest demonstration of it is exactly the comparison where diversity did not move and accuracy halved anyway.
[S] Which is a sentence I would happily paste into every benchmark paper's review form.
[O] So what carries forward into how we build evaluations now?
[G] Three things, and I would keep all three separate from any claim about model capability. First, near-duplicate density is measurable cheaply, before you ship a benchmark. You do not need a held-out model to tell you your test set repeats itself. Second, measure it in more than one slice. Whole corpus, train versus test, and within the test set alone give three different answers, and this paper is the demonstration of that. Third, and this is the one people skip: if you change what is in your evaluation set, you have changed scope and difficulty simultaneously, and you have to separate them or your headline number means two things at once.
[S] And what explicitly does not carry forward?
[G] Any inference at all about modern models. Every solver here is pre-large-language-model. Two statistical systems, and one small tree-structured network that is not a language model. The paper offers no data point on how a modern language model would score on AS Div, and none should be read into it. The mid-thirties accuracy describes a solver landscape the field has long since left behind.
