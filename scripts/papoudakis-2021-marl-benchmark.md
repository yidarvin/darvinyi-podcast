[O] Walk us through the nine algorithms.
[G] Three families. Independent learning, where each agent runs a single-agent algorithm and treats the others as part of the environment. That is independent Q-learning, independent A2C, and independent PPO. Then centralised training with decentralised execution, which splits in two. Centralised policy gradient methods pair a decentralised actor with a critic that sees joint information during training. That is MADDPG, COMA, multi-agent A2C, and multi-agent PPO. And value decomposition, where a joint Q-value is factorised into per-agent utilities. That is VDN, which assumes a linear sum, and QMIX, which learns a mixing network constrained so the joint arg max matches the per-agent arg maxes.
[S] Nine algorithms, and exactly two of them are value decomposition. That matters for how we read any claim about the paradigm.
[G] It does, and I would hold onto that for the whole episode. Whatever this paper shows about value decomposition, it shows about VDN and QMIX specifically. It is not a survey of factorisation methods.
[O] And the tasks?
