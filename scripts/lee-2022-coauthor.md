[G] Two of them, and the paper is precise about how each fails. The traditional HCI approach is contextual inquiry. [G] Right. Every action is recorded as an event, and an event is formally a tuple of an event name, a timestamp, and a snapshot of the current editor state. Table 1 lists them. [G] Then events get compressed into event blocks, which are a deterministic, non-overlapping abstraction over event sequences. Two consecutive text inserts become one insert block. [S] And on top of blocks sit the two collaboration scores, which I found the most interesting piece of engineering in the paper and also the most contestable.
[G] Equality and mutuality, adapted from Storch's work on human collaborative writing from 2002. Equality is redefined as how evenly the writer and GPT-3 distributed turns. [G] Exactly. Mutuality uses two different sets. I, for interaction, contains insert, choose, reopen, and navigate. A, for writing alone, contains dismiss, insert, and delete. Mutuality is the count of I blocks over the count of I plus A.

[S] It also means a writer who never touches the tab key does not score zero on mutuality. They score whatever the insert-over-insert-plus-dismiss-plus-delete ratio gives them.
[G] That is a fair reading of the formula as printed.
