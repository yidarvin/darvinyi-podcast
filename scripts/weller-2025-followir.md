[S] Fine. But narratives alone still do not measure instruction-following, they just make the queries harder. How do they close that gap?
[G] By pairing. Two expert annotators take each narrative and alter it, adding constraints that narrow the definition of relevance. [O] Imogen, that sounds like it should require re-judging an entire collection.

[O] Walk us through the metric itself. Slowly, because I think this is the part people get wrong.
[G] The authors set three requirements. It should compare the original instruction's ranking to the altered instruction's ranking. [S] And the sign convention?
[G] Here is the thing to hold on to. Every alteration in this benchmark makes a previously relevant document non-relevant. So the correct behaviour is always to push that document down. [O] That is genuinely counterintuitive, because in retrieval a positive delta normally means something moved up.
[G] Which is exactly why it gets misreported. Positive here means down, for the specific documents the alteration disqualified.
